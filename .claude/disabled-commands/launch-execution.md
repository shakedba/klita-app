# The Launch Playbook

This resource guides product launches using core frameworks.

## April Dunford's Positioning Framework

Focuses on five essential elements:
1. Identifying your target customer
2. Defining the category
3. Acknowledging competitors
4. Articulating unique value
5. Providing proof points

## Launch Structure

Organize announcements around:
1. The problem being solved
2. How your solution works
3. Why customers should care
4. Clear next steps for adoption

## Key Tools

- **Positioning template** for clarifying market fit and differentiation
- **Launch checklist** covering pre-launch preparation, day-of execution, and post-launch tracking
- **Distribution strategy** spanning email, social, in-product messaging, and press

## Launch Checklist

**Pre-launch:**
- [ ] Positioning finalized
- [ ] Messaging aligned across channels
- [ ] Distribution plan ready
- [ ] Success metrics defined

**Day of:**
- [ ] Announcement sent
- [ ] All channels activated
- [ ] Team on standby for issues

**Post-launch:**
- [ ] Metrics tracked
- [ ] User feedback collected
- [ ] Iteration plan in place

## Notable Insight

As April Dunford notes: "The best positioning makes it obvious who should buy, what they're buying, and why it matters."

Successful launches require clear positioning before creation of marketing materials, ensuring every component reinforces a cohesive value proposition.
