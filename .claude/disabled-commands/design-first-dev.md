# Design-Led Development: Craft Quality Standards

## Core Philosophy

This skill guides when to invest in design quality versus shipping quickly. The framework draws from two leaders:

**Brian Chesky (Airbnb)** emphasizes staying in design details: *"Leaders are in the details. The question isn't whether to be in the details, but which details matter."* His approach prototypes full experiences—including all states—before engineering begins.

**Dylan Field (Figma)** argues that craft quality creates competitive moats: *"With AI, everyone can build. The differentiator is craft. Quality is the new moat."* He distinguishes between features where polish matters versus those where speed wins.

---

## When to Invest in High Craft

Polish these experiences:
- **Core product loops** - Your main value proposition
- **First impressions** - Onboarding, signup, landing pages
- **Frequent interactions** - Daily or weekly use
- **Brand differentiators** - What makes you unique
- **Conversion moments** - Where quality drives business results

Ship quickly for:
- Internal tools and admin panels
- Rapid experiments and prototypes
- Supporting features (non-core)
- Behind-the-scenes infrastructure

---

## Design-First Process

**Before building:**
1. Prototype the complete flow in design tools
2. Design all states: loading, error, empty, success
3. Get cross-functional feedback (design, engineering, marketing)
4. Ensure the feature fits your product narrative

**During development:**
- Build what was designed (avoid "polish later")
- Maintain spacing, typography, and interaction standards
- Use existing design system components

**Key insight:** Working prototypes beat requirement documents. Show, don't tell.

---

## Product Coherence

Chesky advocates: *"Every feature connects to a narrative."* Before shipping anything, ask: Does this strengthen our core story, or add noise?

This prevents feature bloat and keeps customer communication aligned with product direction.
